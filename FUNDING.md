# MISP funding and sustainability

MISP's long-term viability is supported by a diversified funding model. The
project combines public, international, and private sources so that continued
development does not depend on a single funder or company.

## Funding streams

MISP development, maintenance, community support, and related activities are
supported through a changing combination of:

- **CIRCL national funding**, which supports CIRCL's public-interest mission and
  its long-standing work on MISP;
- **European Union project co-funding** for eligible research, development,
  deployment, interoperability, and community activities;
- **international co-funding** through collaborations with public-interest and
  partner organisations in multiple countries; and
- **private partnership funding**, including [MISP Professional
  Services](https://www.misp-project.org/professional-services/), which enables
  organisations to obtain professional support and expertise while contributing
  to the sustainability of the wider project.

The exact mix can vary between funding periods and projects. Diversification
reduces reliance on any one stream and helps sustain the core software,
security maintenance, standards work, documentation, training, and community
infrastructure over the long term.

## Independence and open-source safeguards

Funding does not confer ownership of the MISP community codebase, privileged
licensing rights, or unilateral control over project decisions. Funded work is
subject to the same public governance, contribution, review, and licensing
rules as other work in this repository. Project priorities continue to account
for the needs of the broader MISP user and contributor communities.

MISP does not require copyright assignment or a Contributor License Agreement.
Contributors retain copyright in their contributions and license them under the
[GNU Affero General Public License version 3](LICENSE), as described in the
[CLA-free contribution model](GOVERNANCE.md#cla-free-contribution-model). This
shared authorship prevents a funder, service provider, or private company from
unilaterally converting the complete community codebase into proprietary
software.

Commercial services and partnerships complement rather than replace the freely
available project. The source code remains available under AGPL-3.0 regardless
of which funding stream supported a particular contribution. This separation
between funding and ownership is central to MISP's stability, independence, and
long-term open-source mission.

For more information about MISP and its ecosystem, visit the
[MISP project website](https://www.misp-project.org/) or
[CIRCL](https://www.circl.lu/).
